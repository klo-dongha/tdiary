wehahee ("we", "us") makes the iPhone app "T's Diary" (Korean name: T의 일기, "the app") and runs the web page that hosts this policy ("the site"). This policy explains what personal information is involved, where it goes, and what you can do about it. The Korean version is the original; where the two differ, the Korean version prevails unless the law of your country requires otherwise.

The app fills the top of each diary page with facts about your day: the neighbourhoods you stayed in, sleep, steps and workouts, photos, calendar events and the weather. The app processes this information on your iPhone. We run no server and have no accounts, and we never receive your diary, location, health, photo or calendar information. Your diary and each day's summary (neighbourhood names, times, event titles, weather) are stored on your iPhone; if you use iCloud Backup or back up to a computer, they are included in that backup like other app data. Precise locations and health records are kept out of backups, and visit coordinates are deleted after 14 days (the locations of a home, workplace or school you register yourself are kept only on your iPhone until you delete them, also out of backups). The app sends something to Apple in two cases only: when it looks up the weather, the centre point of your neighbourhood and the date (WeatherKit); and when you type an address to register a place, the letters you typed and an approximate area (the country, or the nearby city when abroad), for Apple Maps search. Deleting the app deletes its data from your iPhone, but backups made earlier may still contain it.

3. Information that leaves your iPhone

  • Weather lookup. To show a day's weather, the app sends Apple's WeatherKit the centre point of the neighbourhood where you stayed longest that day (an administrative centre point, not your actual position; abroad, the centre of the city) and the date. Like any internet request, this reveals your device's IP address to Apple. We do not receive the request or the result. The app keeps a reduced summary (one of five conditions plus the high and low temperature) with that day's diary. To stop weather lookups, turn off "Weather" or "Places" in the app's Settings › What to record.
  • Address search. Only while you type in the address box on Settings › Frequent places › Add (and when you pick a suggestion), the app sends Apple Maps search (MapKit) the letters you typed (or the suggestion you picked) and an approximate area (the country, or the nearby city when abroad) and receives address or building/school-name suggestions and the picked place's position. The area covers the whole country where you last stayed if the app has that country's neighbourhood map (currently Korea only); elsewhere it is about 400 km around the centre of the city where you last stayed. If you have no recent stays or "Places" is off, no area is sent. Like any internet request, this reveals your device's IP address to Apple. It sends nothing at other times and never your actual position (if you register with "Where I am now", the iPhone reads your position once and sends it nowhere). We do not receive the request or the result. The picked position and address text are stored only on your iPhone (section 2). If you do not register by address, nothing is sent.
  • Exports you start. If you tap Settings › My records › Export, the app writes your diary and daily summaries (neighbourhood names, event titles, weather, and sleep and steps only if you turn on "Include health values") as text and hands it to the share sheet, where you choose where it goes. It contains no coordinates. The app never sends it by itself, and we do not receive it.
  • Nothing else. The app has no account, analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking tools and does not use the advertising identifier.

4. Emails you send us

If you email us, we process your email address, your name if you give it, and the content of your message, to answer you and to handle privacy requests. Legal basis in the EEA and UK: our legitimate interest in answering you (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)), or steps you ask for (Art. 6(1)(b)). We keep emails for 1 year after your request is resolved, then delete them, unless the law requires longer. Our mailbox is provided by Google LLC (Gmail).

7. How long we keep information

  • Emails: 1 year after your request is resolved, then deleted.
  • On your iPhone: we hold nothing. Your diary stays until you delete it or the app; visit coordinates are deleted by the app after 14 days (for a day whose summary is not yet made, right after it is made). Places you register (home, work, school) are not subject to the 14 days — they stay on your iPhone, out of backups, until you delete them, use "Delete location records" or "Delete all data". Your edits and each day's summary stay with your diary.
  • Deleting the app removes its data from your iPhone. Backups made earlier may still contain your diary until you delete them in iCloud settings or on your computer.

8. Your choices and rights

  • On your iPhone you are in control. Edit or delete diary entries in the app; delete location records (visit coordinates and the places you registered) or all data in Settings › My records ("Delete location records", "Delete all data" — this cannot be undone); undo your edits to neighbourhoods and hidden places with "Reset places" there, or delete one registered place in Settings › Frequent places; turn items off in Settings › What to record (the app stops reading them); and switch off Location, Health, Photos or Calendar access in iPhone Settings at any time.
  • For emails we hold, you can ask us to access, correct, delete, restrict or stop processing them, to give them to you in a portable form, or object to processing, by writing to wehahee@gmail.com. We answer without delay and within one month at the latest (10 days for requests under Korean law).
  • Complaints. You can complain to the data protection authority where you live or work. In the EEA, see the list of authorities at edpb.europa.eu; in the UK, the Information Commission (formerly the ICO, ico.org.uk); in Korea, the bodies listed in Article 14 of the Korean version. We would appreciate the chance to resolve your concern first.
  • We make no automated decisions about you and do no profiling.

9. Children

The app does not collect children's personal information; everything it processes stays on the device. If a child emails us, we use only what we need to reply. Where the law requires a parent's or guardian's consent (for example under 14 in Korea or under 13 in the United States), we will ask for it or delete what we do not need to reply.

10. Security

We run no server, so there is no database of users to breach. The app's only internet connections are the encrypted weather lookup, which uses a neighbourhood centre point instead of your real position, and the address search on the Add place screen, which sends only the letters you type and an approximate area (the country, or the nearby city when abroad). Precise coordinates (including places you register) and health values are excluded from backups, and visit coordinates are deleted after 14 days. You can lock the app with Face ID or your passcode; the app never accesses biometric data. The only personal information we hold is email, handled by the privacy officer alone.

12. Health data

Sleep, steps and workouts are read from Apple Health only to show them in your diary on your iPhone. They are never sent to us or anyone else, never used for advertising, and never sold. Sleep and step values are kept in a file excluded from backups. See also our Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy (for Washington, Nevada and similar laws).
